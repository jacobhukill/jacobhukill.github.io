Job Market Paper

“Financing Human Capital Acquisition With Student Debt” (Coming Soon)

I develop a life-cycle model of educational attainment, major choice, and student debt repayment to study how the structure and availability of student debt repayment plans impact human capital acquisition and the welfare of individuals. I first examine how the current system of income-driven repayment shapes both the decision to pursue college and the choice of major. I then evaluate the new Repayment Assistance Plan, comparing its effects on enrollment, major selection, repayment behavior, and welfare relative to the existing system.

Working Papers

“The Marginal Propensity to Consume Out of Student Debt Relief” (2025)

Abstract: I calculate the marginal propensity to consume student debt relief in a quantitative precautionary savings model. The fiscal expense of debt forgiveness is large and immediate, while the benefits free resources from recurring future debt payments, which reduces stimulus effects. In response to a one time transfer, the MPC is about 1.5 times higher when targeting indebted households rather than targeting all households. The MPCs of debt relief are less than one third that of one time cash transfers, and less than one fifth that of cash transfers to indebted individuals.
